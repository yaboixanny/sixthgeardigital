01 / THE CHALLENGE

Give every buying situation a clear next step.

The page should help a buyer understand fit and help your sales team start with useful information.

01 / CHALLENGE

Every visitor gets the same journey

A generic homepage and contact form cannot explain every service or buying situation equally well. Page structure should reflect the task the buyer is trying to complete.

02 / CHALLENGE

Technical detail is difficult to use

Large catalogs, buried capabilities, and unexplained terminology slow evaluation. Important information needs a clear hierarchy and a usable mobile layout.

03 / CHALLENGE

Forms create more questions than answers

Too little context leaves sales chasing the basics. Too many questions can make an initial inquiry harder than it needs to be. The form should collect enough for the next step.

03 / BUYING SITUATIONS

Three page types. Three different sales conversations.

Match the journey to the buyer’s urgency, technical needs, and decision process.

Dispatch page

Prioritize the service area, actual availability, phone contact, and equipment scope. Keep the first step quick for a buyer dealing with downtime.

  • Location
  • Equipment & issue
  • Urgency & callback

Equipment quote page

Help a buyer compare the equipment and explain the application. Collect fit and timing details that support an informed quote.

  • Model or equipment category
  • Application & capacity
  • Purchase or rental timing

Project inquiry page

Support a longer evaluation with capability information and project context. Gather the initial brief before moving to technical review.

  • Scope & intended outcome
  • Site or delivery location
  • Project stage & schedule

06 / QUESTIONS & ANSWERS

Before we get started.

Can you work with our existing website?

Yes. We can assess whether focused improvements or new landing pages would meet the need before proposing a full rebuild. The right scope depends on the current system and content.

Can we accept CAD drawings through a form?

That needs a separately agreed file-handling process. We first establish your technical, contractual, and security requirements, then select an appropriate transfer method. A general marketing form is not automatically suitable for controlled files.

Will changing the website affect existing search traffic?

A rebuild should account for existing URLs, useful content, internal links, metadata, and redirects where URLs change. We plan those details and verify the launch rather than treating migration as a visual change alone.

What happens after someone submits an RFQ?

That is part of the project scope. We agree on the receiving team, fields, notifications, CRM requirements, and confirmation behavior, then test the intended path.
